Your accounts

You sign in on the platform's own page (TikTok, YouTube, Instagram or Facebook) and approve the connection there. The app never sees your password. The access token it receives is stored on your own computer.

Nothing is posted unless a person chooses to post it. The app does not read your feed, your messages or your browsing activity, and it does not post on a schedule you did not set.

Why it asks to read as well as publish

For the accounts you connect, the app also asks for read-only access to that account's own details and its own performance figures. On YouTube that means two things and no more:

  • See your YouTube account: the channel's name, its video list and its public counts, so a video the app published is matched to the right channel.
  • See your YouTube Analytics reports: views, watch time, traffic sources and audience figures for that channel's own videos.

These are read-only and cannot change, upload or delete anything. A channel can be connected for analytics alone, with no ability to publish to it. A channel connected for publishing also lets the app add the song's lyrics to an uploaded video as subtitles, and post a reply to a comment when a team member presses Reply. It never edits or deletes anything on the channel.

The figures stay on the Mac that read them and are shared only with members of the same team who have access to that artist. They are not sold or used for advertising. Disconnect any account inside the app, or revoke access from the platform's own settings (for Google accounts, at myaccount.google.com/permissions). The Privacy Policy lists everything that is stored and where.
